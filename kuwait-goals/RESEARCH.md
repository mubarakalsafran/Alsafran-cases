## The 2040 targets (S1, pp. 24–25)

Reproduced exactly as published. The atlas prints each stream's split as a donut
beside a written target line.

| Stream | Recycling | Other recovery | Disposal | Written target line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Municipal solid waste | 30% | 35% | 35% | "Waste hierarchy for municipal solid waste (30% recycling, 35% recovery)" |
| Construction & demolition | 15% | 30% | 55% | "Construction & Demolition waste: 15 % recycling, 30 % recovery" |
| WEEE (e-waste) | 50% | 30% | 20% | "Safe disposal of WEEE (50 % collection rate)" |
| Sewage sludge | 80% (soil application) | 20% (energy recovery) | — | "Sewage sludge: 80 % soil application (recycling), 20 % energy recovery" |

**The WEEE discrepancy is real and is surfaced on the page, not resolved.** The
written target calls 50% a *collection rate*; the donut labels 50% as *recycling*
within a 50/30/20 split. The atlas does not reconcile the two, so neither does the
page: the dashboard card uses the written target's wording, the stacked chart shows
the published split, and a note under the chart states that both appear in the source.

Other targets quoted on the page, all from the same list of 25: the landfill ban on
high-organic-content waste; rehabilitate authorised landfills; rehabilitate harmful
unauthorised dumpsites; prevent further unauthorised waste dumping; separate
collection of hazardous household waste; industrial hazardous waste (safe disposal,
<1% export, 45% landfill); 100% incineration of medical hazardous waste; and the four
data targets under objective 5, including the 31 December 2026 deadline.

## Measured reality (S1, reference years 2018–2020)

- Total waste generated 2018: **37–43 million tonnes** (a range, because parts are estimated)
- Household share: **3–4%** (1,439 thousand tonnes)
- Treatment of 44,654 thousand tonnes: municipal landfills **47%**, clay backfilling **32%**, **recycling 11%**, sand for landfill construction **9%**, three treatment routes at **<1%** each
- Recycled by material: minerals 3,372 kt, metal 1,144 kt, paper 118, glass 60, used mineral oil 44, plastic 35, end-of-life vehicles 35, cardboard 9, batteries 3, others (WEEE, wood, cartridges, UCO) 1 — total 4,822 kt
- Landfill estate 2020: 19 major sites, 6 in operation, **19.8 km²** of land containing waste — closed 9.6 km² (48%), in operation 8.3 km² (42%), **rehabilitated 2.0 km² (10%)**
- Landfill gas ≈ 47,500 m³/h nationally; three municipal landfills emit 96% of it (45,500 m³/h)
- MSW per capita **1.6 kg/person/day** vs GCC 1.5 and global 0.74; household alone 0.85
- Household waste composition: others 26.2%, organic 25.8%, plastics 16.7%, paper & cardboard 12.1%, fine fraction 6.7%, compounds 5.9%, metals 2.9%

Derived on the page: minerals + metal as a share of all recycled material,
(3,372 + 1,144) / 4,822 = **93.7%**, rendered as "94%". This is the only arithmetic
performed on S1's figures, and it divides two numbers from the same table.

## Indicator 12.5.1 (S2, unit confirmed by S3)

All seven years CSB publishes, unmodified:

| Year | 2015 | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Value | 2.94 | 1.24 | 1.05 | 1.50 | 1.10 | 0.81 | 0.76 |

**The unit needed a second source.** CSB publishes the series under two column
headings only — "Year" and "Data" — and states no unit, while the indicator's name
("tons of material recycled") suggests a tonnage rather than a rate. Kuwait's own VNR2
settles it (S3, p. 73):

> In 2015, Kuwait recorded a good performance in terms of recycling solid wastes
> indicator 12.5.1, achieving recycling of **2.94 per cent of the total solid waste
> generated**. This effort however, requires further boosting, as the percentage went
> down to **0.76 per cent in 2021, the lowest rate recorded**.

The page shows both: the unit as CSB publishes it (none) and the unit as Kuwait's
VNR2 defines it (per cent of total solid waste generated).

Also from S3: **SDG 12 achievement 28.9%** as of 2019 (Table 2, p. 5).

From S5: indicator **12.6.1 "Number of companies reporting sustainability"** renders as
a single row with a blank year and a value of 0 — i.e. no national figure is published.
The page states this as an absence of data, never as "zero companies report".

## Comparisons: what was allowed and what was refused

Only two pairs share a definition, a category and a method, so only two progress
comparisons are drawn:

1. **Landfill area rehabilitated** — 10% rehabilitated (2020) against a strategy target
   to rehabilitate authorised landfills and harmful dumpsites. The target sets no
   percentage; the page says so and draws 100% as stated intent, not as a published figure.
2. **Indicator 12.5.1 against itself** — 0.76% (2021) against 2.94% (2015). Same
   indicator, same publisher, same method. Drawn on a 0–3% scale, which the page labels.

Refused, and shown on the page as refused: 30% MSW target vs 0.76% national rate
(different denominators); 30% MSW target vs 11% overall rate (different scope); 15% C&D
target vs recycled minerals tonnage (the source publishes no C&D recycling rate); and
the 50% WEEE and 80% sludge targets, which have no published baseline at all.

## The company dataset

14 large companies operating in Kuwait, researched against their own published
sustainability and ESG disclosures, verified September 2026.

**The sample is not representative and the page says so.** Companies were selected
*because they publish*, which is why 100% of them publish a sustainability report —
that figure describes the selection, not Kuwait. Small, unlisted and family-held firms
are absent by construction.

A practice is marked present only where a specific published claim supports it. Absent
means "not found in published disclosure", never "does not happen".

Counts (n = 14): recycling programme 13 · plastic reduction 8 · paper recycling 6 ·
e-waste recycling 6 · waste separation 3 · sustainability targets 9 · sustainability
report 14. These are computed from the dataset at runtime rather than typed into the
page, so the headline percentages cannot drift from the matrix below them.

### Claims corrected during verification

Two search-derived claims did not survive a check against the primary source, and were
changed rather than shipped:

- **Agility** — a "zero waste-to-landfill by 2026" target was attributed to Agility. The
  company's own environmental page attributes it specifically to **Menzies Aviation**, an
  Agility company, and for **cargo waste**. The dataset now says that, and notes the target
  is not Agility's Kuwait operations. Claimed plastic and steel recycling capacities for
  a "Material Recycling Company" were not on the source page and were dropped entirely.
- **Zain** — the 2030 waste-elimination goal and the Kuwait tonnages were confirmed
  directly against the Zain Sustainability Report 2024, which also supplied precise
  quarterly figures (20,946 kg paper, 1,086.5 kg plastic, 326 kg wood, 107 kg e-waste)
  in place of the vaguer summary.

## Deliberately excluded

- **Kuwait Vision 2035 / New Kuwait waste figures.** The official MOFA page returned HTTP
  403 and no other government-published numeric waste target for Vision 2035 was
  obtained. Secondary sources describe a "sustainable living environment" pillar and a
  "28 targets" figure that conflicts with the atlas's own "25 targets"; rather than pick
  one, the page cites the atlas directly and omits Vision 2035 numbers.
- **A national carbon-neutrality year.** Reported by secondary sources as 2060, but not
  confirmed from a Kuwaiti government publication during this research, so it is not on
  the timeline.
- **Any municipal-solid-waste recycling rate for Kuwait today.** No official source
  publishes one. The page states the absence instead of estimating it from the
  material-by-material table.

## Chart colour validation

The three data series (recycling / other recovery / disposal) were validated with the
`dataviz` skill's palette validator rather than chosen by eye, against the actual card
surfaces used in each theme:

- Light, surface `#FFFFFF`: `#1baf7a`, `#2a78d6`, `#eb6834` — all checks pass, all pairs.
  One contrast warning (`#1baf7a` at 2.82:1) obligates relief, which the page ships as
  visible value labels beside every bar plus a full table view.
- Dark, surface `#191B12`: `#199e70`, `#3987e5`, `#d95926` — all checks pass, all pairs,
  including contrast.

Values were moved out of the bar segments and into colour-keyed chips beside each bar
because white-on-fill text failed contrast on the two lighter series, and ink-on-fill
would have had to flip per theme. In the chips the numbers wear text tokens and the
colour chip alone carries identity.

    ♻️ Official Kuwait waste strategy

    The Kuwait National Waste Management Strategy 2040

    Kuwait’s waste-management goals are not aspirations scattered across speeches. They sit in a single strategy — KNWMS 2040 — developed by the Kuwait Environment Public Authority over several years under the eMISKWaste project, and published with its targets, its deadlines and the authority responsible for each one.

    The vision, in the strategy’s own words

    The five-step waste hierarchy

    Recycling is the third choice, not the first

    The strategy is built on a ranked hierarchy. Prevention beats reuse, reuse beats recycling, recycling beats recovery, and everything beats disposal. A target of 30% recycling is therefore a floor on the third-best outcome — not the ceiling of Kuwait’s ambition.

                  The question this section exists to answer

                  Can Kuwait Reach Its Recycling Goals?

                  On the evidence collected here, the honest answer is: not on the current trajectory — and the reason is not mainly the targets, it is the measurement.

                  Kuwait’s targets are real, dated and owned. The strategy names the responsible authority for each of its 25 targets and sets 2040 as the horizon, with 2030 as a waypoint. That is more structure than a great many national waste plans have.

                  But three things in the record cut against the goals being met on time. First, the one indicator Kuwait publishes over a run of years — the national recycling rate — is falling, not rising: 2.94% in 2015 down to 0.76% in 2021, described in Kuwait’s own review to the UN as “the lowest rate recorded”. Second, the measured baseline is far from the destination: 11% of all waste recycled in 2018 against high-recycling countries at around 50%, with 47% of waste going to municipal landfills. Third, and most telling, Kuwait cannot yet check its own progress against most of these targets — there is no published baseline for the 50% e-waste collection target, none for the 80% sludge target, and no published municipal-solid-waste recycling rate to set against the 30% target. The national total itself is a range, 37 to 43 million tonnes, because parts of it are estimated.

                  That last point is why the December 2026 data deadline matters more than it looks. A target you cannot measure cannot be managed, and Kuwait has given itself a hard date to fix exactly that. Whether Kuwait reaches its 2040 goals is, on this evidence, still open — but whether it will be able to tell is decided in 2026.

                  ✓ What points toward yes

                  • A published national strategy with 25 targets, named owners, action plans and a 2040 horizon.
                  • A hard, dated commitment to publish waste data by 31 December 2026 — the precondition for everything else.
                  • Financing instruments already named: public-private partnerships, extended producer responsibility, gate fees, RDF revenue.
                  • A landfill ban on high-organic-content waste that targets the three sites emitting 96% of Kuwait’s landfill gas.
                  • Real private-sector motion: of — researched companies, most run recycling programmes and several now separate e-waste through the same local facility.

                  ✗ What points toward no

                  • The national recycling rate fell in five of the six year-on-year changes on record, to its lowest value in 2021.
                  • 11% of all waste recycled, and 94% of that is just minerals and metal — paper, plastic and glass barely register.
                  • No published baseline for the e-waste, sludge or municipal-recycling targets, so progress toward them cannot currently be verified.
                  • Kuwait scores its own SDG 12 achievement at 28.9%, and publishes no count at all for companies reporting on sustainability.
                  • Waste separation at source — which the strategy says its success depends on — is the least common practice in the researched company sample.

                  This answer is assembled only from the sources listed below. It is not a forecast, and no official Kuwaiti source states a probability of meeting these targets.

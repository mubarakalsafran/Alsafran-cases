The Gold
Route

Saffron was the most valuable thing that ever crossed this water.

Not oil. Saffron. Gram for gram it was worth more than gold, and it moved the way everything precious moved — by sea, through Kuwait, in the hands of people who knew what they were carrying. A dhow left with pearls and came back with saffron, silk and indigo. A whole coast was built by people who understood one thing: what you carry says who you are.

ALSAFRAN is a family name that happens to mean saffron. We took that literally.

We make the object you carry more than any other — the one thing that's in your hand in every photo, on every table, in every majlis, every lecture hall, every dressing room — and we treat it like it deserves it. Not a plastic shell with a print on it. A finished object: milled buttons, a magnet you can feel, a matte coat that doesn't go shiny in a Kuwaiti summer, and an Arabic letterform drawn by someone who actually knows how Arabic letters connect.

We release in drops, not seasons. When a drop is gone, it's gone — because the person who bought it should be able to walk into The Avenues and not see three of the same case.

How we make it

Four things
we don't move on

Protection is the minimum

Nothing ships until it survives ten drops from two metres onto concrete, 200 button presses, a denim rub and three days on a dashboard in July.

Arabic is not a decoration

Every Arabic design is drawn or licensed properly, kerned by hand, and signed off by a native reader before tooling. No mirrored text, no disconnected letters, no Latin fonts faked into Arabic shapes.

Scarcity we actually honour

We publish the run size before a drop opens and never print another unit. Every limited case is numbered inside the shell and on the box.

Answer in an hour

WhatsApp replies inside 60 minutes, 10 AM to 10 PM, in whichever language you wrote in. Defects replaced immediately, no argument.

The founder

Why I started this

I kept buying cases that were either good or good-looking, never both — and every Arabic design I found had the letters wrong. So I started a brand that treats a phone case the way a label treats a piece of clothing: designed, made properly, released in a run, and then finished. Everything we make starts here, on this coast, and it's built to travel. Like the cargo it's named after.

— Mubarak Alsafran, Kuwait

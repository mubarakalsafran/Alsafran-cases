# 1. Brand Identity

## 1.0 The name you already have — and why it's better than you think

**ALSAFRAN / الصفران.**

Your family name is doing something most startup names can't buy: it sounds like a *house*,
not a shop. Fashion is full of surname brands — Prada, Gucci, Tommy Hilfiger, Elie Saab,
Ashi. Nobody thinks "Prada" describes a product. That's the point. A surname brand can move
from phone cases to sunglasses to luggage without ever renaming itself, and it can never be
copied, because nobody else owns it.

It also carries a gift. **"Safran" is the word for saffron** in French, German, Turkish,
Swedish, Persian and Malay. Saffron is the most expensive material on earth by weight, it is
gold-coloured, and it travelled the exact trade routes that made Kuwait a port. So the name
reads to an international audience as *precious, gold, warm, considered* — and to a Gulf
audience as *family, heritage, qahwa, hospitality*. One word, two truths, zero explanation
needed. That is a rare asset and it should drive the entire visual identity.

**Recommended naming architecture:**

| Layer | Name | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Master brand | **ALSAFRAN** (الصفران) | Wordmark on packaging, site, all official comms |
| Monogram / streetwear mark | **SFRN** | Embossed marks, hardware, straps, caps, tonal drops |
| Legal / commercial | Alsafran Trading Co. (or your licence name) | Invoices, licence, bank |
| Product line naming | ALSAFRAN **Core** / **Signature** / **Atelier** / **Pitch** | Tiers and series |

Drop the word "Cases" from the brand mark itself. Use it only as a descriptor where SEO or
clarity needs it (`alsafran.com/cases`, "Alsafran — Cases & Accessories" as the Instagram
name field). The moment "Cases" is inside the logo, the brand can never sell anything else.

## 1.2 Slogan / tagline

**Primary tagline (use everywhere):**

> ## Wear Your Phone.
> **البسه، لا تخبيه**  *(Wear it, don't hide it)*

It reframes the entire category in three words. A case is not protection — it's something you
*wear*. It works on a box, on a hoodie, in a 3-second TikTok caption, and it survives
translation. It also implicitly invites the accessories roadmap (you can "wear" a strap, a
wallet, an AirPods case).

**Supporting lines, for specific jobs:**

| Line | Where it's used |
|---|---|
| *Protection is the minimum.* | Product pages, spec sections, PDP hero |
| *Precious, like the name.* | Brand/About page, packaging insert |
| *Drops, not stock.* | Drops page, scarcity comms, Drop Pass signup |
| *Made for the pocket. Built for the pitch.* | Pitch (football) series only |
| *من الكويت للعالم* — From Kuwait to the world | National Day / heritage capsules |
| *Your name belongs on it.* | Customise page |

## 1.4 Mission, vision, values

**Mission** — To turn the most-carried object in a person's life into the most personal thing
they own, by combining real protection with design worth being seen with.

**Vision (5 years)** — The default Gulf-born accessories label for people under 30: cases,
carry, and everyday tech, recognised outside the region the way Aesop is recognised outside
Australia.

**Values (these are operating rules, not wall art):**

1. **Protection is the minimum.** No design ships that fails a 1.5 m drop test. Style is never
   an excuse for a cracked screen.
2. **Arabic is not a decoration.** If a design uses Arabic, the letterforms are drawn or
   licensed properly and reviewed by someone who reads it. No mirrored, disconnected or
   Latin-faked Arabic. Ever.
3. **Scarcity we actually honour.** If we say 200 units, we make 200 units. No restocks of a
   numbered drop, no "back by popular demand". Our word is the product.
4. **The unboxing is part of the product.** A customer paid for an experience from the moment
   the mailer lands.
5. **Answer in an hour.** In this market, service *is* the brand. WhatsApp replies inside 60
   minutes during trading hours, in the language the customer wrote in.
6. **We don't discount the brand.** We add value (bundles, gifts, early access) instead of
   cutting price. Discounting a fashion object once teaches customers to wait forever.

## 1.5 Target audience

### Primary segments

**A — "The Campus Stylist" (18–24) — 45% of revenue**
GUST / KU / AUK / Box Hill / ACK students, plus final-year high school. Phone: iPhone 13–16,
often a hand-me-down or family-plan upgrade. Spends 5–15 KD on an accessory without asking
anyone. Buys via Instagram DM and TikTok. Owns 2–4 cases and swaps them with the outfit.
**Buys because:** it will be seen — in the passenger seat, on a café table, in a mirror selfie.
**Killer feature for them:** new drops, personalisation, matching their friend group.

**B — "The Young Professional" (24–30) — 30% of revenue**
First or second job, KFH / Zain / Agility / NBK / ministry, or freelance creative. Phone:
current-gen iPhone Pro, bought outright. Spends 10–25 KD and expects it to *feel* right.
**Buys because:** they want to look considered, not loud. MagSafe matters, wallet matters,
finish matters.
**Killer feature for them:** Signature and Atelier tiers, MagSafe ecosystem, matte that stays matte.

**C — "The Football Kid" (14–22) — 15% of revenue**
Lives in the Gulf Cup, the Kuwaiti league, El Clásico and FPL. Wears jerseys as daily clothes.
Spends 5–12 KD, usually pocket money or family.
**Buys because:** the number on the back is *his* number.
**Killer feature for them:** Pitch series, matchday drops, name + number personalisation.

**D — "The Gifter" (18–35, skews female) — 10% of revenue**
Buying for a friend's birthday, a graduation, a sibling, an Eid gift, a "just because".
Spends 12–30 KD and cares about how it arrives more than the product spec.
**Buys because:** personalisation + packaging = a gift that looks like it cost double.
**Killer feature for them:** name personalisation, gift box, gift note, fast local delivery.

### Where they actually are
TikTok (discovery + entertainment) → Instagram (validation, saves, DMs) → Snapchat (Kuwait is
one of the highest per-capita Snapchat markets on earth — do not skip it) → WhatsApp (the
actual conversation and, for many, the actual order). Physically: The Avenues, 360 Mall,
Al Kout, The Boulevard, Salmiya, campus courtyards, specialty coffee shops, Marina Crescent.

### Anti-audience (say no to these on purpose)
Over-40 value buyers, bulk corporate gifting at low margin (until Year 2), and anyone whose
first question is "what's your cheapest one". Serving them will destroy the price ladder.

## 1.6 Brand personality

**Archetype:** The *Creator* with a streak of the *Ruler* — makes beautiful things, and is
quietly certain about standards. Not the Jester (that's a meme brand) and not the Sage
(that's a spec brand).

**If ALSAFRAN were a person:** 26, Kuwaiti, studied design or business abroad and came back on
purpose. Wears a cream crewneck, one gold ring, clean sneakers. Drinks their qahwa without
sugar. Knows the difference between a good matte and a cheap one, and will tell you once,
calmly, without lecturing. Confident enough to say "sold out" and mean it.

**Five personality traits, with the behaviour each one forces:**

| Trait | The brand does | The brand never does |
|---|---|---|
| **Precious** | Talks about materials and finish in specifics | Uses the words "cheap", "sale", "discount" |
| **Composed** | Short sentences. Lots of space. One idea per post. | Emoji-spam, ALL CAPS panic, countdown-timer pressure |
| **Local without costume** | Uses qahwa, sadu, dhow, majlis as *design DNA* | Puts a camel or a falcon on a case |
| **Youthful** | Football, drops, campus, memes in *Stories* only | Tries to be funny on the product page |
| **Generous** | Free stickers, replies in an hour, replaces defects instantly | Argues with a customer in DMs |

## 1.7 Tone of voice

**The rule:** *Fashion brand on the feed. Engineer on the product page. Friend in the DMs.*

**Three registers:**

| Channel | Register | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign / feed / packaging | **Minimal, declarative, confident.** Max 8 words. No exclamation marks. | "DROP 01. GOLD ROUTE. Friday, 8PM." |
| Product page / specs | **Precise and factual.** Numbers, materials, millimetres. Sell by proof. | "1.4 mm raised camera lip. 12 N42 magnets. Tested to 1.5 m on concrete." |
| DMs / Stories / WhatsApp | **Warm, fast, human, bilingual.** Kuwaiti-friendly Arabic is welcome here — and only here. | "وصلك؟ 😍 send us your unboxing and we'll repost you" |

**Arabic voice policy:** Modern Standard Arabic for anything printed or permanent (packaging,
website, campaign lines) — it exports across the GCC. Kuwaiti/Gulf dialect only in Stories,
DMs and TikTok captions, where it builds intimacy. Never mix scripts inside one printed
sentence.

**Vocabulary — use / lose:**

- **Use:** drop, series, capsule, numbered, milled, soft-touch, MagSafe, finish, colourway,
  archive, edition, restock (only for Core), *precious*, *carry*.
- **Lose:** cover, protector, gadget, accessory shop, best price, offer, sale, ASAP, "dear
  customer", "kindly", "premium quality guaranteed", "100% original".

**Copy examples, side by side:**

| ❌ Generic | ✅ ALSAFRAN |
|---|---|
| "Best quality phone cover, available all iPhone models! Order now 🔥🔥" | "GOLD ROUTE. Eleven cases. Friday 8PM. 200 of each." |
| "Buy 2 get 10% off!!" | "Two cases, one box, one price. The Swap Pack." |
| "Customized cases available, DM for price" | "Your name, set properly. Arabic or Latin. 3 days." |
| "Sorry for the delay dear customer" | "Your order's late and that's on us. It ships today and the shipping is on us." |

## 1.8 Visual identity — premium but youthful

The whole system rests on one tension: **quiet luxury structure, youth-culture energy.**
The *structure* (colour, type, layout, packaging) is disciplined and expensive-looking. The
*content* (photography, drops, collabs) is young and fast. Get this backwards and you become
either a boring corporate brand or a loud cheap one.

### Design principles

1. **One accent, held back.** Safran Gold appears on ~10% of any surface. Restraint is what
   reads as expensive; a gold-flooded page reads as a wedding invitation.
2. **Off-white, not white.** Bone (`#F4F1EA`) everywhere instead of `#FFFFFF`. It instantly
   separates you from every Apple-clone accessory store.
3. **Air is the luxury.** Generous margins, one message per screen, nothing crowded. If a
   layout looks 20% too empty, it's right.
4. **Type does the talking.** No illustrations, no icon soup, no gradients-for-fun. Bold
   grotesque + real Arabic type, and photography.
5. **Bilingual by design, not by translation.** Every layout is built to work in Latin and in
   Arabic RTL. Arabic is set as a first-class citizen at equal or larger optical size, never
   as a smaller subtitle.
6. **Shoot it on people.** 60% of imagery has a hand, a pocket, an outfit or a car interior in
   frame. Cases photographed alone on white are a commodity; cases photographed on people are
   fashion.

### Logo direction

**Recommended: a custom-tightened uppercase wordmark + a monogram.**

- **Primary wordmark — `ALSAFRAN`**
  Uppercase, a tight modern grotesque, letter-spaced +40 to +80 units so it reads as a label
  rather than a word. Slightly tightened `A`–`L` pair. Flat, single-weight, no effects, no
  gradient, no tagline locked in. Set the Arabic `الصفران` in a matching contemporary Kufi/
  geometric sans, drawn to the same cap-height rhythm so the two lock up side by side.

- **Monogram — the `A` / `S` seal**
  An interlocking `A`+`S` inside a soft-cornered square, OR the four letters `SFRN` stacked
  2×2. This is what gets debossed into the case, stamped on the box seal, embroidered on a cap
  and used as the app icon / profile picture. It must be legible at 8 mm.

- **Third mark — the Saffron Thread**
  A single hairline stroke with three small strands at the end — an abstracted saffron stigma.
  Used as a divider, a certificate-of-authenticity element on numbered drops, and a print
  detail inside the box lid. This is the detail that makes the brand feel drawn rather than
  templated.

**Logo rules:** clear space = one cap height on all sides; minimum wordmark width 22 mm print /
90 px screen; only three permitted colourways (Ink on Bone, Bone on Ink, Gold on Ink); never
outlined, stretched, rotated, drop-shadowed, or placed on a busy photo without a scrim.

**Practical route:** commission a Gulf or European type-savvy freelancer to draw the wordmark
in Arabic *and* Latin as one system (budget 250–600 KD). Do not buy a $30 logo — the wordmark
is the one asset you can't cheaply replace later.

### Typography

| Role | Recommended (paid) | Free / open alternative | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display / headline (Latin) | PP Neue Montreal · Suisse Int'l · Söhne Breit | **Space Grotesk** · Archivo Expanded | Hero lines, drop titles, product names |
| Body / UI (Latin) | Suisse Int'l · Neue Haas Grotesk | **Inter** · Archivo | Everything else |
| Arabic display | 29LT Zarid Sans · TPTQ Bukra · Swissra | **IBM Plex Sans Arabic** · Almarai | Arabic headlines, packaging Arabic |
| Arabic body | 29LT Bukra · Greta Arabic | **IBM Plex Sans Arabic** | Arabic site copy |
| Accent / numerals | A single condensed athletic face | **Archivo Condensed** | Pitch series numbers, drop counts (`No. 041/200`) |

Rules: **two families maximum** (one Latin, one Arabic) plus the numeral accent. Headlines
uppercase with tight tracking; body sentence case at generous line height (1.6). Never
centre-align a paragraph. Never use a script/handwriting font anywhere.

| Name | Hex | Reference |

| Qahwa | `#5B3A29` | Arabic coffee |
| Pitch Green | `#1B4D3E` | Football turf |

**Usage split:** 60% Ink or Bone · 30% one product colourway · 10% Safran Gold. Accessibility:
maintain 4.5:1 minimum for body text — Gold is an *accent*, never small body copy on Bone.

### Photography & art direction

- **Surfaces:** raw plaster, unfinished concrete, brushed steel, sand, linen, car leather.
  Never marble, never glitter, never a white infinity table.
- **Light:** one hard directional source with a real shadow (Gulf sun at 4 PM), or moody
  low-key with a single gold rim light for drop teasers.
- **Locations:** a car passenger seat, a specialty coffee shop table, a campus stairwell, a
  concrete ramp, the corniche at golden hour, a football changing bench.
- **Casting:** real Kuwaiti/Gulf students and creators, mixed men and women, styled in what
  they'd actually wear. No stock models.
- **Grade:** warm shadows, slightly lifted blacks, muted greens, saturated gold. Build one
  LUT/preset and use it on **everything** — the grade is 50% of why a young brand looks
  expensive.
- **Format discipline:** every asset shot to work as 9:16 (TikTok/Reels/Stories) *first*,
  then cropped to 4:5 and 1:1. Shoot vertical-native.

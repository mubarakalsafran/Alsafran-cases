# 10. Long-Term Vision — from cases to a house

## 10.1 The thesis

A phone case business has a ceiling: one purchase, once or twice a year, from a customer who
has thousands of alternatives. **A carry brand doesn't.**

The strategic move is to stop thinking of the case as the product and start thinking of it as
**the acquisition device for a system**. The case is the cheapest, most-photographed,
most-frequently-replaced object in a person's daily carry. It's the perfect way in — and once
someone owns an ALSAFRAN case with a MagSafe ring in it, every other thing that snaps to that
ring is a natural next purchase rather than a new sale.

```
                   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
                   │            ALSAFRAN                      │
                   │   "Wear your phone" → then everything    │
                   │        else you carry every day          │
                   └──────────────────────────────────────────┘
   PHASE 1              PHASE 2              PHASE 3            PHASE 4
   the case         the Mag system        the daily carry     the label
   ─────────        ──────────────        ───────────────     ─────────
   iPhone cases     wallets, straps       AirPods, iPad,      eyewear, caps,
   Samsung          grips, mounts,        laptop sleeves,     bags, jewellery,
   personalisation  chargers, cables      tech organisers,    fragrance,
                    power banks           bottles, keyrings   collabs, retail
   AOV ~9 KD        AOV ~18 KD            AOV ~28 KD          AOV ~45 KD
```

The single metric that tracks this whole strategy is **average order value**. If AOV isn't
rising each year, the expansion isn't working — you're just adding SKUs.

## 10.2 The roadmap

### PHASE 1 · THE CASE — Months 0–6
**Goal: prove the brand and get to 100+ orders/month.**

- Drop 01 (Gold Route, 11 designs), then drops 02 and 03
- Model coverage: 4 iPhones → 8 iPhones → add Samsung S25/S25 Ultra
- In-house personalisation live (the UV printer)
- Kuwait only. 300+ Drop Pass members, 50+ photo reviews, one sold-out drop
- **Exit criteria:** repeat rate ≥ 20%, positive contribution per order, one drop fully sold out

### PHASE 2 · THE MAG SYSTEM — Months 4–12
**Goal: raise AOV from ~9 KD to ~18 KD without acquiring a single new customer.**

| Product | Retail (KD) | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **MagSafe card wallet** | 9.900 | Highest-attach accessory in the category. Colour-matched to every case |
| **Wrist strap** | 5.900 | Pure fashion object, near-zero engineering risk, huge colour/print variety |
| **Crossbody phone strap** | 12.900 | The "phone as a bag" trend. Genuinely a fashion item — and a walking billboard |
| **Grip / stand ring** | 6.900 | Cheap add-to-cart at the till, 75%+ margin |
| **Car vent mount** | 11.900 | Every single customer here drives. Sells itself |
| **Mag power bank (5,000 mAh)** | 16.900 | Higher ticket, higher perceived tech value |
| **Braided USB-C cable** | 5.900 | Colour-matched to the colourways; a genuine impulse buy |

*Everything colour-matched to the case colourways.* That's the whole trick — "complete the
look" beats "you might also like" every time.

### PHASE 3 · THE DAILY CARRY — Year 2
**Goal: become the accessories brand, not the case brand. AOV ~28 KD.**

| Product | Retail (KD) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **AirPods Pro / 4 case** | 8.900 | Same materials, same colourways, same factory type. The single most obvious extension you own — launch this in Phase 2 if demand shows up early |
| **iPad case / folio** | 24.900 | Students. Higher ticket, and it makes the brand feel serious |
| **Laptop sleeve (13/14/16")** | 29.900 | Campus hero product. Felt or leather, same design language |
| **Tech organiser pouch** | 19.900 | Travel season, gifting, and a great flat-lay content object |
| **Keyring / bag charm** | 6.900 | Leather offcuts from the Atelier line — literally free material, and it's *visible on someone else's bag*, which is advertising |
| **Passport holder** | 16.900 | Kuwaiti summer travel is a cultural constant |
| **Water bottle / tumbler** | 14.900 | Peak campus-visibility product, and a big UGC generator |

Also in Year 2: an **Arabic type archive** as a product line in its own right — the same
lettering across cases, sleeves, caps and prints. That library is a genuine brand asset.

### PHASE 4 · THE LABEL — Years 3–5
**Goal: a lifestyle brand where accessories are one department. AOV ~45 KD.**

- **Soft goods:** caps, tote and crossbody bags, backpacks, hoodies and tees — **as capsule
  drops only**, tied to collections. Never a full apparel range; that's a different business
  with a different cash cycle.
- **Eyewear** — the highest-margin, most brand-defining accessory there is, and the one that
  most convincingly turns an accessories brand into a fashion label.
- **Fragrance / home** (a Safran candle, an oud-and-saffron scent) — extremely high margin,
  extremely Gulf, and the ultimate proof the brand is about a *feeling*, not a product.
- **Jewellery** — a gold Saffron Thread pendant, signet rings with the monogram.
- **Retail:** a pop-up (Year 2) → a shop-in-shop at The Avenues or a concept store (Year 3) →
  a small flagship studio (Year 4–5). A physical space is what makes a brand permanent in this
  region.
- **International:** a GBP/USD/EUR storefront, a UK or US 3PL, design-press coverage led by the
  Arabic typography story, and one or two international concept-store stockists.

## 10.3 Collaborations — the growth accelerator

Collabs are how a young brand borrows credibility, and how the drop system stays interesting
after the novelty of drop 01 fades. **Two to four a year, no more** — a brand that collabs
constantly has no identity of its own.

| Tier | Partner type | Examples | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — start here** | Local creators & artists | A Kuwaiti illustrator, a 3D artist, a calligrapher, a photographer | Cheap, fast, credible; their audience becomes yours; genuinely new design |
| **2** | Subculture institutions | A five-a-side league, a car club, a skate crew, a specialty coffee roaster, a gaming team | Deep community access money can't buy |
| **3** | Cross-category Gulf brands | A Kuwaiti streetwear label, a local perfume house, a Gulf sneaker store, a café chain | Shared audiences, shared cost, double the content, retail distribution |
| **4** | Regional culture | A Gulf musician, a football content creator, a regional artist | Real reach and press |
| **5 — the ambition** | International | A global sneaker/streetwear label, a football club's lifestyle arm, an international artist | The moment the brand stops being "from Kuwait" and starts being "from Kuwait, sold everywhere" |

**The one rule:** every collab must produce a *design*, not a logo swap. A partner's logo
printed on your case is a t-shirt-stall move. A partner's *ideas* changing what the case looks
like is a collaboration.

**The calligrapher collab is the one to do first.** A serious Arabic calligrapher, three pieces,
200 units each, at 19.900 KD. It costs almost nothing, it's beautiful, it is *impossible* for a
reseller to imitate, and it's the story that gets a Gulf brand written about abroad.

## 10.5 What must never change

As the range expands, four things hold the brand together. Break any one of them and the
expansion turns into a general accessories shop with a nice logo.

1. **The drop promise.** Numbered, capped, never restocked. It applies to a hoodie and a candle
   exactly as it applies to a case.
2. **Arabic typography, drawn properly.** This is the brand's most valuable and most exportable
   asset. Every new category inherits it.
3. **Ink · Bone · Safran Gold.** One palette across every category, so a case, a sleeve and a
   bottle photographed together are unmistakably one brand.
4. **The Saffron Thread on everything.** One hairline mark, on every product in every category,
   forever. It's what turns eleven cases and forty accessories into a house.
